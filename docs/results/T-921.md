# T-921 — the check-width floor, re-measured on the **shipped** count

[T-577](T-577.md) measured ADR-0022 §4.3's width floor with a **copy** of the proposed count
living in the harness, and §4.3.1 accordingly allowed `min_check_width = 8` only "once the count
above ships, re-measured by the same harness". The count shipped (T-575, T-928). This ticket
makes the harness run the real one, puts the searched path on it too, and re-measures.

**Verdict: the floor STAYS at 16.** Not because the count failed — it closed both holes it was
written for, and it closed the searched half of the shift artefact completely — but because
re-measuring found a **third** artefact the count does not see, the **burst pair**
`x^a·A + x^b·B`, whose realised rate is `C(M,2)·2^(−2w)` for an emitter with `M` distinct
messages: **1.5 × 10⁻⁵ at w = 8 for the minimal M = 2 (0.31 × the whole budget), 4.2 × 10⁻³ for
an M = 24 window (84 × over), against 6.4 × 10⁻⁸ at w = 16 (0.0013 ×)**. Two further holes
(short idle tail, wide cancelling unit) were found and **closed** here; they were width-
independent, so they bear on correctness rather than on the floor.

The ADR's rule that numbers are never loosened after seeing results is respected: the floor did
not move, and three guards got stricter.

## 1. Method

The harness is `crates/hk-estimate/tests/degenerate_null.rs`, unchanged in design from T-577 §1
and changed in exactly one respect: **it no longer carries its own copy of the count.** The
columns headed `gate|REAL` and `real` are `hk_model::synth::tally::CheckTally` — the same type,
the same call, that the `crc` / `bch` / `parity` / `checksum` blocks run. The type moved from
`hk-blocks/src/evidence.rs` to `hk-model/src/synth/tally.rs` for that reason: `hk-blocks`
depends on `hk-estimate`, so neither the harness nor `assist::codes` could reach the count where
it lived, and a copy is what T-577 was reduced to.

The numbers below come from

```
HK_T577_TRIALS=4000 HK_T577_SEARCH_TRIALS=100 \
  cargo nextest run -p hk-estimate -E 'binary(degenerate_null)' --no-capture
```

(1 113 s, debug). The default run is an ~12-second assertion suite that pins every finding.
Rates are **per window = per confirm decision**; the budget is ADR-0022 §1.2's
`1/N = 5 × 10⁻⁵`. `0 (<x)` is the one-sided 95 % bound `3/n` for zero events.

## 2. What the count closed

| hole | population / cell | pre-§4.3.1 count (`GATE`) | shipped count (`gate\|REAL`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** init-cancel | N2 runs, anchored, init = ones, w = 24 | 1.98e-2 | **0 (<7.5e-4)** |
| **A** init-cancel | N2 runs, anchored, init = ones, w = 32 | 2.03e-2 | **0 (<7.5e-4)** |
| **B** shift, template | beacon \| g divides it, grid, w = 8 | 1.00 | **0 (<7.5e-4)** |
| **B** shift, template | …, grid, w = 12 / 16 / 24 | 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00 | **0 / 0 / 0** |
| **B** shift, **searched** | beacon \| g divides it, grid, every w | 0.20–0.31 (T-577 §2.5) | **0 (<3.0e-2)** |

Both are mechanisms, not rates, and both are gone. The searched row is the one that needed new
code: `assist::codes` now counts the spans of each search cell with the same `CheckTally` and a
span it refuses may not pay for the claim (it is still *tested*, so the look-elsewhere
denominator is unchanged — fewer bits, never more). T-577 §2.5's finding that ADR-0021 §8.2's
null control cannot see this artefact therefore no longer needs the control to see it.

**The trim width is `w`, per candidate width, and getting that wrong costs real codes.** This
ticket's first attempt recorded each span once at the cell's *widest* width, reasoning that a
span which is idle fill under the widest trim is idle fill under every narrower one. That is
backwards: the guard trims `w` bits off each end, so a wider trim leaves a **shorter** span, and
a short span reads constant or two-period far more often — `is_short_periodic` had refused to
look at spans that short at all, which is exactly the hole §4 closes. Measured on random spans,
trimming at 32 rather than at the width under test refuses **75 % of 34-bit, 44 % of 36-bit and
16 % of 40-bit** frames that the `crc` block at w = 8 counts, i.e. precisely the short ISM sensor
frames with a byte-wide check that the search exists to find; on a 24-frame CRC-8 population at
34 bits it took the credited differences from 10 to **6** and the evidence from 64 to 32 bits.
Two things are worth recording about the corrected version:

- **It changes no measured false-confirm rate.** Re-running the whole measurement with the
  per-width trim reproduces the searched table below **row for row**, and the template-fixed
  table is untouched (it never went through `assist::codes`). That is the expected result rather
  than a lucky one: the shift artefact is a *multiple* of one counted burst and dies on the dedup
  rule, which does not depend on the trim width. The over-wide trim was pure collateral damage
  on real frames, buying nothing.
- **The search is not identical to a block's call, deliberately.** A block records
  `ok && upstream_clean`, having a generator to validate against; here the generator is what is
  being searched for, so every distinct span is recorded and `check` intersects that mask with
  the spans that actually validate. The direction is permissive for the dedup and the rank cap,
  and `credit_mask`'s doc comment says so rather than claiming identity.

**Every other cell of measurement 1 is `0 of 4000` at every width** — N1/N3 iid, N2 runs, N3
DSSS, the unseeded beacon, the no-code sensor and the Manchester chips, anchored / jitter / grid,
init 0 and init all-ones. Two cells are nonzero and both are the design working:

- **the period artefact (C)** at w = 4 only (0.94–1.00): a payload repeated at lag `ord(x mod g)`
  passes whatever the payload, and no count removes it. This is T-577 §2.4's "never below a
  byte", unchanged.
- **the seeded shift beacon at w = 24 / 32** (2.5e-4 / 0.995 anchored): at w ≥ 24 a *single*
  valid frame is 24–32 bits, which is what §4.2 asks of it (`min_differences(32, 0) = 1`). The
  realised rate is `2^(−w) ×` the cell: **2.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ at w = 32**, inside the budget by five
  orders of magnitude. It is a confirm from one 32-bit check, not an artefact.

## 3. Why the floor cannot go to 8 — the burst pair

ADR-0022 §4.3.1's dedup rule is "not a multiple or divisor of one already counted", and it is
exactly that: **one** burst. A frame holding two *different* already-counted bursts at arbitrary
offsets is `x^a·A + x^b·B`, which a linear check divides whenever it divides both — valid **by
construction**, a multiple of neither, and (A, B, composite being linearly independent) not
caught by the affine-rank cap either. A two-message emitter that occasionally sends its two
messages back-to-back produces exactly this.

Measured, conditional on the two `2^−w` events (4 000 windows per cell, `gate|REAL`):

| framer | w = 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 24 | 32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| anchored | 0 (<7.5e-4) | **1.00** | **1.00** | **1.00** | 0.999 | **1.00** |
| jitter ≤ 7 | 1.00 | **1.00** | **1.00** | **1.00** | 0.998 | **1.00** |
| grid | 0.327 | 1.12e-2 | 0.630 | 0.905 | 0.998 | **1.00** |
| **searched**, anchored (100 windows) | 0 (<3.0e-2) | 0.96 | 0.95 | 0.96 | 0.94 | 0.94 |

Conditionally certain from w = 8 upward, on the framer a real anchor gives. The prior is the
only thing that prices it, and the prior is `2^(−2w)` per pair of the emitter's messages:

| | realised rate, M = 2 | M = 4 | M = 8 | M = 24 (one per frame) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **w = 8** (`2⁻¹⁶`) | 1.5e-5 (**0.31 ×** budget) | 9.2e-5 (**1.8 ×**) | 4.3e-4 (**8.5 ×**) | 4.2e-3 (**84 ×**) |
| w = 12 (`2⁻²⁴`) | 6.0e-8 | 3.6e-7 | 1.7e-6 | 1.6e-5 (0.33 ×) |
| **w = 16** (`2⁻³²`) | 2.3e-10 | 1.4e-9 | 6.5e-9 | **6.4e-8 (0.0013 ×)** |

At w = 8 the mechanism spends a third of the *entire* budget on the smallest emitter it can act
on, and is over budget from three distinct messages upward. A 902–928 MHz window — the
canonical playground, full of multi-message sensors and remotes — is the M = 8…24 column. At
w = 16 the same mechanism costs one part in 800 of the budget. The crossing is between w = 12
and w = 8, so **8 is not supportable and 16 is comfortable**, which is the same conclusion T-577
reached by a different mechanism.

**A second, independent reason not to move on this run.** The searched half of measurement 1 is
100 windows per cell, so a zero row bounds the cell only at `3.0 × 10⁻²`. Multiplied by the
`2^−w` prior that is `1.2 × 10⁻⁴` at w = 8 — **above** the 5 × 10⁻⁵ budget — and
`4.6 × 10⁻⁷` at w = 16. This run cannot certify w = 8 for the searched path even where it saw
nothing. Certifying 8 would need ~10⁵ searched windows per cell (the searched table already
costs ~15 minutes at 100), and a mechanism-level argument for the pair hole, not more samples.

## 4. Two holes found and closed on the way

Both were found by asking the harness the coordinator's questions from T-575's delta review.
Both are **width-independent**, so neither is a floor argument; both are correctness fixes, and
both make the guard strictly stricter.

- **(C) an idle tail of 16 bits or fewer.** `is_short_periodic` deliberately refuses to look at a
  span of `SHORT_PERIOD_MAX_BITS = 16` bits or fewer — a span that short is trivially "periodic"
  at `p = n`. So for `1^w ‖ 0^m` with a short tail, *none* of `degenerate_frame`'s four trims was
  ever tested, and hole A came back at every w ≤ 16: `1^8 ‖ 0^16` is valid for an init = all-ones
  CRC-8 and counted as an independent trial. `degenerate_frame` now also refuses a span that
  repeats with **at least two whole periods** at any length, which is a structural claim rather
  than a length technicality. New test:
  `hk-model synth::tally::tests::an_init_cancel_frame_with_a_short_idle_tail_is_degenerate`.
- **(D) a cancelling prefix wider than the register.** `CheckTally::record`'s `register_bits` is
  the trim, and the `checksum` block passed its check-field `width`. A sum / XOR /
  ones'-complement checksum consumes whole **units**, so with `unit_bits > width` (a legal
  configuration: `unit_bits ∈ {8, 16, 32}`, `width` defaults to it but may be narrower) the
  shortest prefix that cancels the state is a whole unit, the `width`-wide trim leaves most of
  that unit inside the span, nothing looks idle, and `U ‖ 0…0` counts as an independent trial at
  every width. `checksum` now passes `max(width, unit_bits)`; `parity`, whose register is one
  bit per unit but whose cancelling prefix is likewise a unit, passes `unit`.

The third and fourth items of that review are already covered: **spans with `start_bit ≠ 0`** by
T-928 and by the harness's `start_bit = 8` model (the whole-frame guard demonstrably misses the
frame the covered-span guard catches), and **a frame holding two copies of one burst** by the
divisor rule, asserted in
`hk-model synth::tally::tests::zero_padded_shifts_and_doubled_copies_of_one_burst_are_one_trial`.

**What the (C) fix costs, measured rather than asserted.** Share of *random* frames the guard
refuses, trimmed at w = 8 — the guard before this ticket against the guard after:

| frame length | before (`is_short_periodic` alone) | after (+ constant / two whole periods) |
|---|---|---|
| 34 bits | 43.4 % | 43.4 % |
| 36 bits | 12.4 % | 12.4 % |
| 40 bits | 0.6 % | 0.6 % |
| ≥ 48 bits | 0.0 % | 0.0 % |

So at a narrow width the (C) fix costs **nothing**: the two columns are equal. It bites only when
the trim is wide relative to the frame (at w = 32 the same lengths go 0 % → 75/44/16 %), which is
the honest reading — a 34-bit frame under a 32-bit check has two payload bits — and which is why
the search must trim at the width under test (§2).

**Pre-existing and NOT from this ticket, worth its own ticket:** that 43 % at 34 bits is
`is_short_periodic`'s own rule. It tests periods up to 16 as soon as a span exceeds 16 bits, so an
18-bit span is called "periodic at p = 16" on **two matching bit pairs** — a 1-in-4 coincidence.
The consequence is a false *negative* (a real frame not credited), so it never admits a false
confirm, but it costs a third of the differences on the shortest ISM frames. The two-whole-repeats
rule this ticket added is the shape that fix wants: require `n ≥ 2p` there too, and re-measure the
w = 4…8 column of §2's table, which is the only place a stricter short-span rule could matter.

## 5. What the shipped count costs a real emitter — §4.3.1 corrected

ADR-0022 §4.3.1 says of the count: *"It costs the five real-emitter models nothing: it equals
`differences` at every k."* **That is not true of what shipped**, and it was never measured of
it: T-577's `indep` column was the dedup rule *without* the affine-rank cap. With the cap — which
is the point of §4.3.1's third rule — the count is the rank, and the rank is below `differences`
wherever the payload repeats itself (200 runs, anchored, template-fixed):

| emitter (w) | k | `distinct_valid` | `differences` | **shipped count** | rank + 1 | `assist::codes` D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| beacon, fixed payload (16) | 128 | 1.0 | 1.0 | **1.0** | 1.0 | 0.0 |
| two-message toggle (16) | 128 | 2.0 | 2.0 | **2.0** | 2.0 | 0.0 |
| sensor, 3× repeats (8) | 128 | 17.3 | 17.3 | **7.4** | 7.4 | 7.0 |
| rolling counter (16) | 128 | 128 | 128 | **7.9** | 9.0 | 7.0 |
| DF17-like squitter (24) | 128 | 128 | 128 | **41.0** | 41.0 | 40.0 |

The shipped count **is** `rank + 1`, exactly, up to the 64-frame counting cap (which is why the
counter reads 7.9 against a rank + 1 of 9.0 at k = 128: the ninth basis vector arrives after the
cap — fewer bits, never more). `distinct_valid` and `differences` still differ by 0.0 on every
real emitter, so T-575's swap still costs nothing; what costs is the cap, and the cap is
deliberate: a rolling counter does not present 128 independent trials, it presents nine. Every
emitter still meets §4.2's requirement at its own width from few frames, and the search's `D` is
now exactly one below the block count on every row — the one constant per group it pays, as
§4.3.1 describes, and never above it.

**§4.3.1's sentence should read:** *the count equals `differences` on an emitter whose payload
does not repeat (a squitter below k = 64), and equals the affine rank + 1 — materially fewer —
on one that does. That is the cap working.*

## 6. What goes back where

- **ADR-0022 §4.3 / §4.3.1**: `min_check_width` stays **16**, now re-measured on the shipped
  count and not merely on the shipped count's absence. §4.3.2 records the burst pair and the
  statistical-power limit. §4.3.1's real-emitter sentence is corrected per §5 above.
- **docs/07, docs/21**: the conditional "8 only once §4.3.1's count is re-measured" is
  discharged — it *was* re-measured, and the answer is 16. Both now say so.
- **No code constant moves.** `hk-pipeline` `inventory.rs` and `hk-synth` `engine.rs` are
  already 16 and `SynthesizedConfirm::effective` already clamps a configured 8 back to it.
- **Open, for the coordinator to file (1).** `is_short_periodic` calls an 18-bit span periodic at
  `p = 16` on two matching bit pairs, refusing ~43 % of random 34-bit frames at any width (§4). It
  is pre-existing, cuts the false-negative way only, and the fix is the `n ≥ 2p` rule this ticket
  already wrote for the short-span clause.
- **Open, for the coordinator to file (2).** The burst-pair hole has a candidate fix that this
  ticket did not take: a frame whose nonzero bits decompose exactly into **disjoint** shifted
  copies of already-counted cores is free (a linear scan per candidate, no false refusals — a
  real new payload does not decompose into earlier bursts). Note that the obvious
  generalisation — "refuse anything in the ideal generated by the counted cores" — is **wrong**:
  that ideal is `gcd(counted)`, which collapses to the generator after two real frames and would
  refuse every subsequent genuine trial. If the decomposition rule lands and re-measures clean,
  the w = 8 column above drops to the searched path's power limit and the floor question can be
  re-opened; until then 16.
