# hackriff

An exploration-first signals-analysis tool for software defined radio, meant to replace the HackRF PortaPack: a portable device plus software that **finds** interesting signals instead of making you know the frequency and pick FM/AM/WFM by hand. It serves science (space weather, radio noise, propagation), spectrum awareness and decoding unknown signals, as well as hobby listening.

## Product vision (from the user)

The core workflow, in order:
1. **Peruse.** Browse live airwaves casually, or open reports of past scan surveys.
2. **Automate.** Run configurable, thorough scans across frequency regions on a schedule.
3. **Review history.** Choose a region and see what activity was seen there over time.
4. **Recommend explanations for what was detected.** Signals are always found by **blind detection** from RF data first, then characterized. Only then does the known-signal database (band plans, licences, catalogues) offer **ranked, reasoned suggestions**, e.g. "looks like FM; FM broadcast allocation nearby; 150 kHz off raster".
   - **Never a source of truth.** The database is never the starting point, never pre-populates the inventory, and never overrides what was measured.
   - **Mismatches are interesting.** An emission off its expected frequency (a shifted FM station) is flagged, not snapped to the database.
   - **Unknown signals are the priority** to surface and catalogue. (The one documented exception: GNSS acquisition is known-code-led, confined by ADR-0018.)
   - **Tests:** fixtures carry a hidden ground-truth list of interesting emissions. A test runs blind detection, checks each one is found, and checks a sensible explanation is among the top recommendations. Never look a frequency up in the database and tune there.
5. **Record and stream demodulations.** Modulation, bandwidth, squelch and AGC are estimated from the signal, never picked manually.
6. **Decode to bitstreams.** Blind symbol and bit recovery, including for unknown signals.
7. **Stream bits to other programs**, which turn them into something useful. Decoders are pluggable consumers, not hard-coded apps.

Further goals:
- **A radio "attack map".** Like port-scan and botnet maps: explain *why* your spectrum changed by linking local anomalies to external events (space weather, GNSS jamming, satellite passes, balloon launches, lightning). See docs/05 §3.
- **Science is first-class.** Solar flares, noise-floor studies and propagation, down to what a radio engineer knows but rarely watches.
- **General, not a decoder catalogue.** ADS-B, pagers and radiosondes are the well-known cases, not the point.
- **Tune from the processed output.** Closed-loop refinement: demodulate and decode, measure output quality (discriminator offset, pilot/RDS lock, audio SNR, CRC-valid rate), and adjust centre, bandwidth and other parameters from it. Rough selections and detections are refined automatically, not set by hand.

## Signal & inventory model (invariants, from the user 2026-09-16; recorded in ADR-0017/0019)

These constrain detection, the data model and the UI, and hold across milestones.

- **A signal is a time–frequency region, not a persistent carrier.** Every detection has a centre frequency, a bandwidth **and a time extent** (start + stop, or start + duration). Signals need no carrier and no stable frequency: a 10-second chirp or a one-off burst is a signal, and such **ephemeral emissions are first-class** — never forced into a "steady emitter parked on one frequency" shape. (The 902–928 MHz ISM band — rtl_433 sensors, remotes, LoRa — is the canonical playground: short bursts everywhere.)
- **One unified model: `[start, end?]`, ongoing until an end is found, and the end is revocable.** Note when a signal **starts**, then **assume it continues to the live edge (`end = null`, "ongoing") until an end is affirmatively detected** — "still present" is the default, not something re-confirmed every frame or poll. A "forever" signal is just an ongoing signal that began when that band's recording did and has never been seen to stop; there is no separate persistent-carrier concept. A detected end is **provisional and revocable**: if later samples show the signal resumed within tolerance, **null the end and keep the single interval open** rather than splitting it or spawning a new emitter (generalising the idle-gap / revival model, T-262/T-264). **UI consequence:** an ongoing signal's box is drawn from its start to the live edge by assumption and capped only by a detected end; the client needs only **start / end / reopen** events, never a per-poll presence bump to advance the box top (that per-detection growth was the T-388 jumpiness). Rapidly re-testing presence, or gating the next row on an end decision, are the rejected alternatives.
- **The inventory is time-scoped to the view.** The Explore **Candidate** and **Confirmed** lists reflect only what is *currently identified in the viewed window*, not an all-time accumulator. Each identified region draws a **box** (centre/width/time) on the surface, at its own place on the canvas's time axis; the durable all-time record lives in a separate **history** surface (workflow #3), where ephemera are catalogued as past events with a timespan. Scrubbing re-derives the live lists (recent history comes from the IQ ring).
- **Candidate / Confirmed / History.** *Candidate* = a hypothesis about energy in the current window. *Confirmed* = a verified real emitter, carrying its own time-presence track. *History* = the durable catalogue of every event, including one-offs.
- **Detection is fast, continuous and self-cleaning.** Re-detect rapidly and often; a region's few real signals should resolve quickly (target ~2–10 s), with near-duplicate detections **merged** into one emitter and candidate confidence **decaying / expiring** when a region goes quiet. No unbounded seen-count accumulators, no ghost candidates for signals that have stopped. A persisting signal's box **grows** along the time axis as it continues.
- **Overlap is an error signal that triggers re-analysis.** Real emissions essentially never overlap in time–frequency (two that truly did would not demodulate), so overlapping Confirmed/Candidate boxes are proof the analysis is wrong, with at least one true signal inside the union. The system **detects the overlap and automatically re-analyzes that region** to resolve it to the real signal(s), rather than leaving competing boxes stacked; Confirmed still suppresses overlapping Candidates and overlapping Candidates compete by evidence, but the resolution is active re-analysis, not just ranking. Candidates are expected to churn — created and deleted continuously.
- **Decode operates on a captured region and extends with it.** To decode, capture a time–frequency section and run the pipeline over it; live decoding **extends the region's time extent** and decodes only the newly-arrived part (incremental), never re-decoding what is already done.

## The view: one surface over one (time × frequency) window (invariants, from the user 2026-09-16/17)

Since MCANVAS (docs/16 §8, T-437–T-445) the live waterfall, the history view and the two edge navigators are **one surface**: a virtual canvas with **X = frequency across the full device range (1 MHz–6 GHz), Y = time**, over the coverage tile pyramid, drawn by one WebGL2 context into N scissored **panes** (each with its own centre/span in both axes), with a **minimap** that is just another viewport at a coarse level. **"Live" is the finest-level growing edge where hardware is tuned**, so there is no live-vs-history split to keep consistent. Time↔pixel mapping, pane state and scroll animation are legitimate thin-client presentation (like the pixel↔Hz mapping); the data and its timestamps come from the backend. The rules below all survived the cutover (docs/14 tabulates where each now lives); the old two-bar mechanics did not.

- **The whole UI is scoped to one (time × frequency) window and must show all the data it has for it.** Every surface — the canvas, the Candidate/Confirmed lists, the output/decode panels — is a view over the current selection. **Whenever data exists for that window** (live, the IQ ring, the spectrum-history pyramid, prior surveys) **it must be shown**; a surface may render grey/empty *only* where data genuinely does not exist, never as a fixed-size placeholder, and switching to Live **backfills from history** rather than starting black. The backend keeps a **coverage map derived from the SDR configuration/tune history** (per interval: which centre/span/rate on which device — provenance the data model records), so observed-vs-unobserved is computed from what was actually sampled. "We have it but didn't render it" is a bug. **Grey = genuinely unobserved, and it is the point:** across 6 GHz most of the canvas is honestly empty, and the shape of what is *not* grey is the survey. Observed-but-not-yet-measured and unknown-whether-we-looked are distinct marks, not grey (T-441).
- **One view window, even with multiple SDRs.** Additional SDRs collect passively in the background and widen the coverage available to display; they never add view windows. Panes are where you look *from*, not extra subjects; a pane's `device` only chooses whose coverage decides its grey. The minimap lights every active capture window as a segment from the same coverage the panes read — the natural home for multiple SDRs and for survey/sweep coverage.
- **One shared time axis.** For the view there is one canonical mapping from **absolute capture time** (sample index / ns) to screen position, and **everything time-varying is laid out through it and moves together**: tile rows, every signal box, selections, the time cursor, pane rectangles. Overlays are anchored in capture time, never at fixed screen coordinates, and **re-lay-out on every render frame in the same pass as the data** — never on the data-poll cadence (per-poll boxes against a per-frame scroll is exactly what drifted, then jumped). Every time-varying record the backend serves (spectrum frames, detections, presence extents, boxes) carries absolute capture time. Boxes drifting out of step with the rows is an invariant violation, not a cosmetic bug.
- **Pause freezes the view, not the capture.** Capture, the ring and detection are **always-on**. A pane either **follows** the live edge or is **frozen** on a window; a pane's pause *is* its time window (no separate flag — T-347 retired the run-wide pause), and pausing, scrubbing and zooming never reach a device route and never stop or slow the SDR, the ring or detection — they only change what the screen shows.
- **The capture window lives on the canvas's time axis (T-338, folded in by T-506).** There is no separate capture band any more: the canvas *is* the visualization of the retained capture. The **capture window** is exactly the configured recording/retention duration the backend reports (`GET /api/timeline`'s `window`: the IQ ring's `retention_s`, `t0..t1`), never the longer, lossy spectrum-history horizon, and it grows or shrinks when that duration is reconfigured. Two **rules** across every pane mark it, laid out through the pane's own capture-time mapping on every frame: the **retention bound** (live edge − `retention_s`) and the **IQ horizon** (the oldest sample the ring holds, `buffered.t0`) — visually distinct from the coverage grey and from each other, and stated in words beside them, including whether the pane's own time position still has IQ. The canvas's time extent is **floored at the capture window**, so the whole retained window is always reachable however young the history is, and extends further back only where spectrum history exists (the canvas is also the history view). The capture clock (`state.captureWindow`, T-379's live-edge fallback) is polled from `centre/capture-clock.ts`; nothing on this path may fall back to a default span or a browser clock.
- **Navigation is view arithmetic in time and a device command in frequency.** Drag pans and wheel zooms (both axes; a modifier gives one — T-456), snapping to **realizable** states: in frequency, centre and span are bounded by the instantaneous bandwidth and the tuning step; in time, by the retained window and the pyramid's discrete resolution tiers, with rows-per-second a function of the chosen span so zooming re-scales rather than truncates. **A pan or wheel never commands the radio** (spy-client tests assert an empty call list). Time is always a view over already-captured data, but a frequency outside the tuned window can only be reached by tuning there, so **panning to un-tuned spectrum offers a retune and selecting a region commands one**: a discrete, explicit act through the one gated `DeviceAction` path (one capture at a time, settle gap, `device_id` recorded), snapped to the nearest achievable config, re-derived at the instant of commit and refused if the view moved. An error appears **only when no achievable configuration can capture the region** — centre outside the tunable range, span wider than the instantaneous bandwidth, or outside a replay's extent. The view **opens on the observed extent from the coverage map** (`surface/bootstrap.ts`) — never on the whole 1 MHz–6 GHz range's midpoint, and never derived from `frequency.current` or a band plan — so the tuned window is visible at first paint (the T-376 rule, carried to the canvas).
- **The UI never implies detail the front end can't deliver.** Three honesty tiers — **live-IQ detail**, **spectrum-history**, **survey overview** — stay visually distinct, and each pane states the level it was actually drawn at, so a wide or deep zoom shows overview rather than upscaled detail presented as measurement (exploration-first honesty, applied to navigation).

**Live rendering, tile maintenance and playback (invariants, from the user 2026-09-17).** These are the product model, not tickets; they constrain `hk-store`'s history, `hk-pipeline`'s ingest and the whole client.

- **The live view renders like a classic SDR waterfall: rows append in real time as they are recorded, and live is NEVER gated on batch tile generation.** A live edge that stalls because a tile cannot yet be built from partial data, or waits until a whole tile can be produced, is a **bug**, not a cost of the tile design: the row exists, so "show all data that exists" already requires it on screen. Zoom is unaffected — the rows appended at the finest level are the rows the pyramid is built from.
- **Tiles are maintained LIVE and INCREMENTALLY, per downsample interval, and empty regions short-circuit through the coverage map.** At the finest level rows append to the live tile; a level that downsamples every N rows adjusts its in-progress top row as rows arrive and **commits every N** — the same rule at 2, 4, 8, 16, 32. Coarse levels are incrementally maintained structures, not batch materialize-on-demand work. A tile's default is "no data", and each recorded row's frequency tiles are already known, so **a viewport tile with no data is answered immediately from the coverage map** and never runs the generation path. T-453's constraint still binds and is what to measure against: work on the capture thread is paid whether or not anyone looks, and that thread gates the ring — per-arriving-row cost **must be measured, not assumed**, and residency must not grow back with node count.
- **The spectrum trace is viewport-wide, time-addressable and per-pane** (T-457). The most-recent-sample FFT plot spans **the whole viewport width** as one continuous plot across frequency, rendering only the section shown and only where data exists. Following live it is the top-most sample; exploring the past it is the FFT **at that pane's time position**, placed through the pane's own capture-time mapping. An unobserved span reads as a **gap**, never a zero or a line interpolated across it.
- **Playback moves "now" forward over recorded history: analysis is immutable, demod/decode re-run.** Play from a chosen past time with play/pause, advancing as if live (reusing `SigmfReplaySource`, ring-window extract and T-384). **Detections and history are never re-run** — they replay exactly as recorded; **demod and decode are**, being functions of raw IQ. So playback has **two horizons**: spectrum history is long, but raw IQ exists only in the IQ ring (~30 min) and in recordings. Beyond the IQ horizon playback is waterfall-only with **no audio**, and **IQ availability is its own visible, predictable boundary on the time axis** — the IQ-horizon rule on the canvas, drawn from the same ring window (above), never from the spectrum-history horizon. Promising audio that cannot be delivered is the same defect as implying resolution never captured. A per-pane playhead is deferred.

## Product constraints (decided with the user, 2026-09-13)

- **Form factor: portable handheld.** A PortaPack replacement: PortaPack-sized ideal, cyberdeck-sized acceptable. Smaller than a laptop, not benchtop. A bigger battery and more weight are fine.
- **Scope: one self-contained device.** Networked sensor meshes, cellular linking between devices and fixed home-mounted sensors are **out of scope**. The attack map therefore works from **this device's own survey history** plus **external context feeds** (space weather, GNSS-jamming maps, lightning, TLEs, SondeHub…) whenever connectivity exists. Design offline-first: cache reference data (band plans, licence extracts, TLEs), sync or export when online. Don't design anything that rules out sharing later.
- **Hardware: HackRF One** as the RF front end (1 MHz–6 GHz, 20 Msps, 8-bit, half-duplex, USB 2.0, no preselector). **NVIDIA Jetson** as compute, over a Pi 5 because it costs little more and adds a GPU; likely the Orin Nano (Super) class (the original Nano is legacy) — confirm module, price, power modes and JetPack support before purchase. Low-power operating modes matter.
  - Keep the front end abstracted so HackRF Pro or other SDRs can be added later.
  - **Allow several SDRs at once (future option).** Don't bake a single-device assumption into the source layer, scheduler or inventory: multiple front ends in the one unit may run concurrently, to cover different ranges in parallel or to widen the instantaneous window (contiguous bands stitched non-coherently; true coherent aggregation needs a shared 10 MHz reference via HackRF CLKIN/CLKOUT). Each detection's provenance records which device/antenna saw it. This stays one self-contained unit, not the out-of-scope mesh. 20 Msps ≈ saturates USB 2.0, so concurrent devices want separate USB controllers.
- **Language: whatever has robust support, but signal processing must be fast, so Python is for orchestration and research only, never the real-time path.** (Settled by ADR-0001 and the spikes: Rust core, see Engineering.)
  - **Hard requirement:** change and extend signal-processing pipelines **without stopping capture or rebuilding**. The user's GNU Radio experience was endless recompiling.
- **UI: settled and shipped as a web thin client** (TS+WASM; ADR-0002/0013). Don't re-raise native.
- **Licence: undecided, and not a development concern for now.** Don't let licences gate work.
- **Team: one developer**, with one likely friend-user. Favour low operational complexity; don't over-build plugin infrastructure for hypothetical contributors.

## Key findings that constrain the design

- **Sweep to find *where*, dwell to find *what*.** A HackRF sweeps 0–6 GHz in under a second but misses short bursts; the scheduler balances survey against real-time dwell windows of up to ~20 MHz.
- **Classical DSP first, ML later.** The big wins are noise-floor estimation, CFAR plus spectral kurtosis, calibration and spur rejection, blind symbol-rate/frequency-offset/bandwidth estimation, and licence/band-plan priors. ML classifiers lose accuracy over the air and can't reliably say "unknown": use ML as a stage after normalization with an explicit unknown/open-set output. The Jetson GPU makes that stage feasible.
- **The front end limits what automation can trust.** An 8-bit HackRF without preselection mostly shows intermodulation in cities. Detections need provenance: gain state, overload flags, spur masks. A switched filter bank is a likely hardware add-on.
- **No open-source tool covers the whole chain** (survey → detect → classify → estimate → decode → inventory → record). Reuse proven decoders and ideas rather than rewriting them.
- **Many use cases fall outside 1 MHz–6 GHz** (VLF/ELF, Ku-band) or need TX. Model receiver capabilities explicitly and mark what the base device can't do.

## Test strategy direction

Items in `docs/05` / `docs/use-cases.yaml` are acceptance targets: map each ID to the capabilities it exercises and tag whether it is testable **offline with recorded or synthetic IQ** or needs live hardware.
- Prefer **SigMF** recordings plus annotations as fixtures (own captures, IQEngine/SigMF archives, sigidwiki samples, synthetic via TorchSig / generated).
- End-to-end tests replay IQ through the full pipeline and assert on detections, estimated parameters, decoded bits and inventory entries, blind against a hidden truth list (above). Unit tests cover the DSP blocks.
- **End-to-end tests drive the system through the SDR device interface**, never feeding files straight into the pipeline. **A mock SDR device** implements the same interface as the real HackRF source (tune, sample rate, gains, bias-tee, sweep, start/stop, timestamps, overruns) and replays recorded SigMF IQ behind it, honouring retunes and gain/rate changes realistically. **The same tests run against the real HackRF** as hardware-in-the-loop tests.
- **Keep the device interface generic** so other SDRs (e.g. SoapySDR) can be added later; don't bake HackRF specifics into the core.

## Engineering

Layout and build come from `docs/12` and the ADRs.

- **Layout:** Cargo workspace in `crates/` (`hk-model` data model + SQLite; `hk-core` source/ring/scheduler; `hk-dsp` spectral/noise/channelizer; `hk-detect`; `hk-estimate`; `hk-demod`; `hk-store` history+SigMF; `hk-context` feeds+priors+correlation; `hk-api` control API (re-exports `hk-stream`); `hk-stream` stream-output contract; `hk-plugins` plugin host; `hk-pipeline` composed pipeline; `hk-cli`; plus `hk-blocks`, `hk-recipe`, `hk-classify`, `hk-ml`, `hk-gnss`, `hk-sim`, `hk-synth` decoder synthesis (MAUTO, ADR-0015)). `plugins/` decoder manifests+wrappers; `ui/` TS+WASM web client; `py/` synthetic-gen/fixtures/research (orchestration only, never the real-time path); `spikes/` throwaway; `fixtures/` SigMF (Git LFS / external store); `tests/` e2e IQ-replay.
- **Languages/licence rule:** Rust core, C-via-FFI liquid-dsp (MIT), CUDA (Jetson-only, behind the `gpu` cargo feature), TS+WASM UI, Python tooling. **GPLv3 code (VOLK, GNU Radio, most decoders) stays behind the plugin process boundary** (ADR-0010). The dependency licence ledger is optional bookkeeping, not a gate.
- **GPU work is Mac-first.** New GPU work implements the Mac provider (wgpu/Metal or Accelerate) behind the compute-provider conformance suite first; CUDA ports come in the Jetson phase.
- **Build/test/run:** `just build`; `just test` (T1 unit + T2 component + T3 replay + T4 synthetic; no hardware; `gpu` off); `just replay <fixture.sigmf-meta>`; `just deploy-jetson` (rsync + on-device build). CI's two jobs each call **`just gate`** (`--phase check` / `--phase acceptance`) with no hardware and must stay green. HIL (T5) runs on a bench rig nightly/manually; field (T6) is logged, never gates CI.
  - **Testing (T-077/T-436):** `just test` runs the Rust suite through cargo-nextest; heavy/timing-sensitive tests (hk-pipeline listen/retune/lossless/refine/stream, hk-api, hk-cli, hk-core ring stress/concurrency, hk-demod::refine_wfm_real) are pinned to a serial `heavy-serial` group in `.config/nextest.toml`. **tests/e2e (hk-e2e) runs under nextest too, since T-631** — `just acceptance`, `just e2e-harness` and `just acceptance-m*` all go through `just _e2e-run`, and hk-e2e sits in its own `e2e-bounded` group at **max-threads = 6**, the concurrency T-603 measured; over the acceptance gate's 82 tests that is 390 s on the old `cargo test` path, 1133 s serialised at 1, and **230 s at 6**. Before T-631 that override named `heavy-serial` and had never applied to a single test, because no nextest invocation could see the package — `just nextest-config-check` now fails on exactly that shape. `heavy-serial` sits inside a profile that caps `test-threads = 8` for every nextest run (the default of one per logical CPU produced different timing failures on each run while four agents held 24 of 28 cores; CI overrides via `NEXTEST_PROFILE: ci`). The cap reduces false reds; it is not a fix for the timing-bound tests (T-430/T-433/T-446). **Timing-based tests are two kinds (docs/10 §3.6, user 2026-09-22):** one that reads the wall clock to choose *what* to assert on is a bug — fix it by reading the instant off the wire and pinning the index, never by deleting it; one whose *assertion* is a latency/throughput bound lives in the **`timing` tier** — `.config/nextest.toml`'s `default-filter` keeps it out of every gate run and **`just timing`** runs exactly that set, one at a time, on a quiet box or nightly, like HIL. `just test-seq` is the sequential `cargo test` fallback; `just acceptance` (the M0 slice suite) is its own step, matching CI's `test`/`acceptance` split. **Agents working a single task run targeted tests, not the full suite:** `just test-crate <crate>`, or **`cargo nextest run -p <crate> -E 'binary(<name>)'`** for one binary. **Avoid `just test-one <name>`:** it is a WORKSPACE-wide operation — it builds and `--list`s every test binary (~280) before running one, and under load it was measured spending **over 8 minutes without reaching a test**, spawning ~58 concurrent list processes, where the scoped `nextest` form did the same job in **7.5 s** (T-489). Its filterset also traps: `test(<file>)` matches nothing and exits non-zero — use `binary(<file>)`, and the `test(/regex/)` form when a name needs matching.
  - **The merge gate is one command: `just gate` (T-396).** It inspects the diff (merge base with `main` plus anything uncommitted; `--base REF`, `--staged`, `--worktree`, `--files …` override), **classifies** it, **prints the decision and the deciding files**, and runs exactly what that class needs: `ui/` only → `just test-ui`; anything touching `crates/` or `docs/api.md` → `just lint` + `just test` + `just acceptance-ci`; `docs/` only → nothing (no link checker or markdown linter exists, and the gate says so); `py/` only → `just lint-py` + `just test-py`. `--dry-run` prints the decision; `--phase check|acceptance` runs half the chosen suites, which is how CI's two jobs split one decision. The rule lives in the runner, not in each agent's judgement, so the same diff gets the same gate whoever runs it.
    - **The coordinator merges with `just gate-merge` (T-424),** run inside `git merge --no-ff --no-commit`: it classifies **the merge index** (what the merge will actually put on `main`) rather than the working tree, so main's permanently-untracked `tools/` and diagnostic `fixtures/` captures no longer force every merge to the full gate. It is a narrowing, not an exemption: it requires `MERGE_HEAD`/`SQUASH_MSG` or forces the full gate, prints every uncommitted path it did not classify with the class it would have had, and has **no ignore list** — `fixtures/` or `tools/` staged into a merge is still full. Reasoning in `docs/10`.
    - **Classification fails closed.** A path matching no class runs the **full** gate, never the cheapest — the same principle as `BiasTee::Unknown` is not `Off` and `Coverage::Unobserved` is not quiet. So `fixtures/`, the `justfile` and `.github/` (the gate must not certify its own weakening), `tests/`, `plugins/`, `.config/`, `recipes/`, `Cargo.*`, repo-root files and any new top-level directory are all full. The classifier is a pure function in `py/hkpy/gate.py`, tested class-by-class in `py/tests/test_gate.py`. `just lint` + `just test` + `just acceptance-ci` by hand stays the periodic/milestone check.
    - **Affected-crate selection is an AGENT ITERATION convenience only — the merge and CI gates always run the whole workspace (T-543, corrected 2026-09-20).** `py/hkpy/crates.py` computes the reverse-dependency closure from `cargo metadata` at target level — a package's tests link its lib deps *plus* its dev-deps and each dev-dep's own lib closure — and can pass it to `lint-rust`/`test-rust`/`test-doc` through one variable, `HK_GATE_CRATES`. But that computation is now **opt-in only**, via `just gate --select-crates`, and `py/hkpy/gate.py`'s `resolve_selection` refuses it unconditionally — before even looking at the flag — for `just gate-merge` (`--merge`) and for `just gate` running inside CI (`GITHUB_ACTIONS`), so neither path can be narrowed even by an accidental future caller. `just gate` and `just gate-merge` with no extra flags (what CI, the coordinator and `ops/merge-runner.sh` all actually run) are therefore always full. Measured closures, for the opt-in case: `hk-cli` 2 of 20, `hk-pipeline` 3, `hk-api`/`hk-plugins` 4, `hk-detect` 5, `hk-dsp` 14, `hk-model` 20. **Unset (the default) means the whole workspace**, so every way this path can fail lands on the expensive answer. `Cargo.lock`, `Cargo.toml`, `.config/`, `fixtures/`, `plugins/`, `recipes/`, `.github/` and any path in the Rust tree belonging to no package force the whole workspace regardless; `just gate --no-select` does too, on demand.
    - **The gate times itself, and `just cycle-time` reads it back (T-543).** Every run appends its class, phase, per-suite durations, exit code and load average to `$HACKRIFF_OPS/gate-timings.jsonl` (`py/hkpy/gatelog.py`), with the start line written *before* the first suite so a killed or starved gate still leaves a trace. `just cycle-time` prints that plus branch cut → queued → merged from `ops/merge-runner.log` and git. **First measurement, 2026-09-20: gate median 21.4 min, but queue wait median 119.7 min and commit→merge median 272.6 min — the gate is ~8 % of a ticket's cycle.** Run it when the loop feels slow, instead of guessing.
      - **Compare like with like, and per suite — `just cycle-time --suites` (T-763).** That 21.4 min is *all classes pooled*, passing gates only, so a 17-second `py` gate and a 40-minute `full` one are in one median; it is **not** comparable to the `full`-class figure the budget guard reports, and reading the two against each other is most of the "60 % slower in two days" of 2026-09-22. The honest like-for-like drift over the 24 complete passing `full` gates was **30.0 → 34.7 min (+16 %)**, and it is **one step**: the workspace suite went 462 → 756 s at the 09-21 04:55 gate on +8 tests, when four `hk-classify` sweep binaries and `hk-estimate::receiver_lines` each got ~3× dearer. Two rules the tooling now enforces: an **aborted run is not a measurement** (the gate stops at the first failure — half of all full gates never reached the UI e2e suite), and **classes are never pooled**. The per-suite history lives in `ops/merge-runner.log`, not in `gate-timings.jsonl`, which had 31 of its 33 records written by the test suite itself. Detail and the table: `docs/10`.
    - What no gate covers: a client asking the backend for **the wrong thing**. Contract tests assert the *server* serves a route correctly, not that the client calls it correctly (T-367 requested `/api/timeline` with no band and drew an empty canvas while every suite stayed green). The guard lives in `ui/test`: **assert the request the client builds**, not only the response it renders.
- **Adding a use case:** append an ID in `docs/05` and `use-cases.yaml` (never renumber), set `capabilities`/`hardware_fit`/`accessory`/`fit_flags`/`fit_note`/`test_tier` per `docs/06 §3` and `docs/10 §2`. **Adding a fixture:** capture/annotate as SigMF, put small ones in `fixtures/` (LFS) or the external store, reference it from the acceptance test by use-case ID.
- **The board is edited through `just task …`, never by hand** (user, 2026-09-22: "they should actually be banned from editing tasks.yaml directly" — direct hand-edits broke the board's strict YAML twice that day). `py/hkpy/tasks.py` edits one ticket's block with text-level operations, the same discipline as `hkpy.boardmerge`/`hkpy.reconcile`, and re-validates every write (`yaml.safe_load` + unique ids), restoring the original bytes and exiting non-zero if a write would break it. Subcommands: `just task show T-nnn`; `just task list [--status S] [--milestone M] [--ready] [--group G] [--json]`; `just task set T-nnn key=value ...` (refuses an unknown status and refuses `status=blocked` with no `blocked_on`); `just task result T-nnn (--from|--text)` (replaces the `result:` block); `just task note T-nnn (--from|--text)` (appends to the `notes:` block); `just task new --title T --milestone M [--priority P] [--model M] [--effort E] [--group G] [--depends-on T-a,T-b] [--use-cases X,Y] [--acceptance-file …] [--notes-file …] [--found-by S]` (allocates the id from every local branch tip plus the working file, never reusing one); `just task validate`. A `PreToolUse` hook (`.claude/hooks/block-board-edits.sh`, plus a redirect/`sed -i` pattern in `block-full-gate.sh`) denies direct `Edit`/`Write`/`MultiEdit`/shell edits to `docs/tasks.yaml` in worktrees; `HK_ALLOW_FULL=1` overrides it for a genuine debug/repro agent.

## Coordination

The multi-role workflow — **supervisor**, **coordinator**, **pipeline manager**, and spawned **workers** — is defined under `.claude/`, so each role loads only the rules it needs (root `CLAUDE.md` holds the shared invariants every role inherits):

- **Coordinator** operating rules — `just reconcile` each tick, delegate → gate → merge, model/effort tiering per ticket, triage-before-quarantine, batch ready branches, never park a merge, manage tickets (never just report): `.claude/roles/coordinator.md` (launch in-role via `ops/launch.sh coordinator`).
- **Supervisor** rules — relay + verify each landing, heartbeat, guaranteed-task discipline, verify before asserting, the full stop: `.claude/roles/supervisor.md` (`ops/launch.sh supervisor`).
- **Pipeline manager** (2026-09-23) — owns **throughput**: the ops daemons (`merge-runner`, `work-runner`, `watchdog`, `monitor`, `stage`), their knobs, holds, experiments and code; measures with `just flow`, changes one knob at a time through `just experiment`, never runs a merge or gate by hand, never lowers gate honesty, never pauses dispatch for measurement, holds the queue at most 30 minutes: `.claude/roles/pipeline-manager.md`, invariants `.claude/rules/pipeline-invariants.md`, workflows `.claude/pipeline/workflows/` (`ops/launch.sh pipeline-manager`, one instance). The coordinator owns the backlog, the supervisor the user's intent, the runners the mechanics — this role owns the rate.
- **Worker / reviewer / deflaker / capture-agent** subagents the coordinator spawns via the Agent tool: `.claude/agents/*.md` (worker runs targeted tests only and hands back — a PreToolUse hook blocks the full gate in worktrees).
- **Procedures** (merge-gate, deflake-triage, reconcile, file-ticket, capture-sigmf): `.claude/skills/`.
- **ONLY THE COORDINATOR ALLOCATES TICKET IDS (T-841, decided 2026-09-22).** Every session used to compute "next free id" as `max+1` over the board **it can see**, which is its own branch; two sessions filing concurrently therefore pick the *same* ids. That happened five times on 2026-09-22, the largest 26 wide (T-761..T-772 on coordinator branches against T-761..T-786 filed on `main` from `docs/26`, renumbered under duress to T-800..T-825), and the duplicate-key guard in `py/tests/test_task_board.py` only fires *after* the merge that creates the duplicate — by which point the ids are already in commit messages, cross-references and agent briefs. **So: one writer.** No session but the coordinator invents a `T-NNN`. Any other session — worker, reviewer, deflaker, supervisor, or an interactive session of the user's — that finds something worth a ticket **describes it in prose with the exact evidence, in its result/handback**, exactly as the existing worker filing rule already says, and the coordinator allocates the id and files it. Amending an *existing* ticket needs no allocation and is unaffected. This needs no new tooling and matches how the board is already treated (managing tickets is the coordinator's job, not merely reporting them). The disjoint-range convention — the coordinator handing an agent its own block of ids at spawn — stays valid, and is the same rule: the id still comes from the coordinator. The evidence is that on 2026-09-22 **no collision came from an agent; all five came from two sessions.**
  - **The coordinator allocates from `main`'s board plus its own in-flight branches** (`git show main:docs/tasks.yaml` and each branch tip it has out), never from one worktree's view.
  - **What still fails:** two *coordinator* sessions running at once re-open the race exactly as before — don't run two; and a ticket that lives only in a result file is not on the board until the coordinator reads that result, so an unread handback loses it (a visible, recoverable loss, unlike a duplicate id). Filing directly on `main` from a non-coordinator session — the `docs/26` case — is now simply banned.
- **The main checkout belongs to the merge runner — this binds EVERY session that opens it, not only the coordinator.** Small board/docs edits go straight to `main` as edit → check → commit in one step, and only when `.git/MERGE_HEAD` is absent and no bulk gate is running (`$HACKRIFF_OPS/bulk-in-progress`); the full rules are in `.claude/roles/coordinator.md` ("Merging"). Never leave an uncommitted file there, and never `git stash` or `git reset` there: on 2026-09-22 an interactive session's `stash` erased the runner's staged merge (it then committed a single-parent, 4-of-44-file "merge", ea91c27c), and its uncommitted test was wiped by the runner's `reset --hard` rewind of a failed batch — the rewind protects commits, not edits. Code the gate would test goes on a branch in its own worktree and through `merge-queue.txt`; if you cannot commit to `main` right now, that is where your edit goes too. Test a board edit against a copy (`git show HEAD:docs/tasks.yaml`), never by stashing.
